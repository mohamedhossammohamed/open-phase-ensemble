1. Takens' Embedding Theorem & Phase Space Reconstruction

Floris Takens (1981) established that the unobserved multi-dimensional attractor manifold $\mathcal{M}$ of a non-linear dynamical system can be reconstructed from a single observed scalar time series $v_t$ via delay coordinate vectors:

$$\mathcal{X}_t = [v_t, v_{t-\tau}, v_{t-2\tau}, \dots, v_{t-(d-1)\tau}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^d$$

where $\tau$ is the time delay lag and $d$ is the embedding dimension. If $d > 2m + 1$ (where $m$ is the manifold dimension), the delay embedding map $\Phi: \mathcal{M} \to \mathbb{R}^d$ forms a smooth topological immersion that preserves original manifold geometry.

2. Parameter Selection: AMI and FNN

Average Mutual Information (AMI)

Fraser & Swinney (1986) demonstrated that the optimal time delay $\tau$ corresponds to the first local minimum of the Average Mutual Information $I(\tau)$:

$$I(\tau) = \sum_{x, y} P(v_t, v_{t+\tau}) \log_2 \left( \frac{P(v_t, v_{t+\tau})}{P(v_t) P(v_{t+\tau})} \right)$$

This selects a lag $\tau$ where scalar coordinates $v_t$ and $v_{t+\tau}$ are maximally independent in an information-theoretic sense.

False Nearest Neighbors (FNN)

Kennel et al. (1992) introduced False Nearest Neighbors to determine the minimal embedding dimension $d$. Points projected into dimension $d$ may appear adjacent due to geometric overlap rather than manifold proximity. The fraction of FNNs is tracked as $d$ increases:

$$\frac{|v_{t+d\tau} - v_{nn+d\tau}|}{\|\mathcal{X}_t^{(d)} - \mathcal{X}_{nn}^{(d)}\|} > R_{\text{tol}}$$

The optimal dimension $d$ is identified where the FNN fraction falls below $1\%$.

3. Covariance Geometry: Ledoit-Wolf Shrinkage

When sample window size $N$ is small relative to embedding dimension $d$, the standard sample covariance matrix $\Sigma_{\text{sample}}$ is ill-conditioned. Ledoit & Wolf (2004) analytical shrinkage computes a well-conditioned estimator $\Sigma_{\text{LW}}$:

$$\Sigma_{\text{LW}} = (1 - \delta) \Sigma_{\text{sample}} + \delta \mu I, \quad \mu = \frac{\text{trace}(\Sigma_{\text{sample}})}{d}$$

where optimal shrinkage intensity $\delta \in [0, 1]$ minimizes expected Frobenius loss, guaranteeing positive-definiteness and invertibility. The robust Mahalanobis distance is evaluated as:

$$D_{\text{mahal}}(Z_t) = \sqrt{(Z_t - \mu_Z)^T \Sigma_{\text{LW}}^{-1} (Z_t - \mu_Z)}$$

4. Surrogate Null Models: AR and IAAFT

To test whether dynamic predictability exceeds linear autocorrelation noise, surrogate null series are generated:

  • Autoregressive (AR) Surrogates: Linear stochastic processes preserving spectral covariance.
  • Iterative Amplitude Adjusted Fourier Transform (IAAFT): Schreiber & Schmitz (1996) phase-randomization algorithm that exactly preserves both the empirical amplitude distribution and Fourier power spectrum of the original series.

5. The 6-Detector Battery

The ensemble combines six mathematically orthogonal detectors to score each incoming streaming observation:

1. Simplex Projection (EDM)
Sugihara & May (1990) Simplex Projection performs non-parametric forecasting by tracking trajectories of $E+1$ nearest phase-space neighbors: $$w_i = \exp \left( -\frac{\|Z_t - Z_{nn_i}\|}{\|Z_t - Z_{nn_1}\| + \epsilon} \right), \quad \hat{v}_{t+h} = \sum_{i=1}^{E+1} \bar{w}_i v_{nn_i + h}$$ Anomaly score $s_t$ measures normalized prediction error $|v_t - \hat{v}_t|$.
2. Robust Mahalanobis (Ledoit-Wolf)
Measures geometric spatial deviation from the robust covariance centroid in projected space.
3. Subsequence Motifs (STOMP Matrix Profile)
Yeh et al. (2016) Matrix Profile computes z-normalized Euclidean distance from every subsequence window $W$ to its nearest non-overlapping historical neighbor. Peaks identify structural discords.
4. Subspace Isolation (Isolation Forest)
Liu et al. (2008) Isolation Forest isolates point anomalies via axis-aligned random feature splits. Path length $h(x)$ across trees measures anomaly severity: $$s(x, n) = 2^{-\frac{\mathbb{E}(h(x))}{c(n)}}$$
5. AR Linear Ridge Filter
Online AR(p) linear ridge regression filter measuring stochastic prediction residuals.
6. Transformer Autoencoder (MSE Reconstruction)
Lightweight PyTorch Transformer Encoder ($L=2$ layers, $H=4$ heads, $D_{\text{model}}=32$) acting as a sequence autoencoder: $$s_t = 1 - \exp\left(-\frac{1}{L \cdot D} \sum_{l,d} (x_{l,d} - \hat{x}_{l,d})^2\right)$$

6. Meta-Judge Online Fusion: Hedge Algorithm

Freund & Schapire (1997) Hedge multiplicative weight update algorithm updates expert weights $w_{t, k}$ based on trailing correlation loss $\ell_{t, k} = 1 - \text{PearsonCorr}(S_k, E_k)$:

$$w_{t+1, k} = \frac{w_{t, k} e^{-\eta \ell_{t, k}}}{\sum_{j=1}^K w_{t, j} e^{-\eta \ell_{t, j}}}$$

Fixed-share mixing (Herbster & Warmuth, 1998) guarantees a weight floor $w'_{k} \ge \frac{\sigma}{K}$ ($\sigma = 0.01$) to prevent expert weight collapse during sudden regime shifts.

9. Zero-Lookahead Leakage Invariant

The pipeline strictly guarantees zero-lookahead temporal isolation:

  • Causal Buffer Scope: All transformations operate exclusively on historical causal buffers $W_t = \{x_1, x_2, \dots, x_t\}$. No future sample is ever accessed.
  • Invariant Verification: tests/integration/test_no_lookahead.py confirms that element-by-element streaming execution yields an exact Euclidean distance of 0.0000 compared to sequential batch processing.

open-phase-ensemble

An open-source, non-parametric, multi-tool ensemble for streaming time-series anomaly detection and forecasting.

Preliminary Research Self-Reported Metrics

Time-series anomaly detection suffers from methodological fragmentation and evaluation pitfalls. Many academic benchmarks rely on flawed point-adjustment protocols that artificially inflate random guessers to >90% accuracy. Meanwhile, heavily parameterized deep neural models remain opaque, computationally expensive, and sensitive to hyperparameter tuning.

open-phase-ensemble takes a fundamentally different path: constructing an open-source, non-parametric streaming pipeline that maps scalar temporal observations into phase-space manifolds via Takens' delay embedding, combined with a battery of six orthogonal expert detectors governed by an online multiplicative-weights Meta-Judge.

System Foundation & Architecture

Rather than attempting to train a single deep network to recognize all possible anomaly signatures, the system decomposes time-series evaluation into six modular, causal compartments:

Non-Parametric Foundation
Reconstructs underlying phase-space manifolds via Takens' delay embedding instead of training millions of opaque parameters.
Orthogonal 6-Detector Battery
Combines phase-space trajectory prediction, covariance geometry, subsequence motifs, subspace isolation, linear autoregression, and neural MSE reconstruction error.
Online Meta-Judge
Employs the Hedge multiplicative-weights algorithm with fixed-share mixing floors to dynamically reweight experts in real time without ground-truth labels.
Zero-Lookahead Invariant
Enforces strict element-by-element streaming execution. No future data points are ever exposed during online scoring.
Methodological Rigor
Evaluated using un-buffered Volume Under Surface (VUS-ROC/PR) metrics with label-only buffering and IAAFT phase-randomized surrogate null baselines.

The Research Journey

The genesis of open-phase-ensemble began with a detailed audit of a closed-source time-series verification engine. That reference demonstrated a compelling premise: raw time-series observations could be mapped into multidimensional phase-space manifolds using Takens' delay embeddings to predict future system dynamics via geometric trajectory matching.

However, as we audited the mathematical bounds of pure phase-space trajectory matching, a critical structural limitation emerged: while trajectory projection excels at capturing non-linear dynamic shifts, it struggles with spatial covariance shifts, variance drops, and isolated global point outliers.

"No single detection algorithm can be universally optimal across all anomaly types. True robustness demands an ensemble of mathematically orthogonal experts."

To solve this, we designed a battery of six complementary algorithms:

  • Simplex Projection (EDM) — Phase-space trajectory geometry & non-linear prediction
  • Robust Mahalanobis (Ledoit-Wolf) — Covariance geometry & variance shift detection
  • Matrix Profile (Single-Window) — Subsequence motif & discord discovery
  • Isolation Forest — Subspace partitioning & global point anomalies
  • AR Linear Ridge Filter — Linear stochastic prediction residuals
  • MSE Transformer Autoencoder — Neural sequence reconstruction error

Choosing to Build Openly

We chose to develop and release this research entirely in the open for three primary reasons:

  • Auditable Reproducibility: Closed-source benchmarks cannot be independently audited. Open code guarantees that every score, calculation, and claim can be verified.
  • Metric Integrity: By adopting Volume Under Surface (VUS-ROC/PR) with label-only buffering, we eliminate point-adjustment inflation.
  • Community Collaboration: A clean, modular design allowing researchers to plug in new detectors, surrogate generators, or gating strategies.

Significance & Scientific Outlook

The observations documented in this repository are offered as directions for further investigation, not as established conclusions. We believe this work demonstrates that an open, composable, online-learning ensemble can achieve meaningful predictive edges over phase-randomized null baselines while remaining fully inspectable and deterministic.

Key open research questions include:

  1. Ensemble Scalability: How does the Hedge Meta-Judge scale as the detector battery expands to 20+ experts?
  2. High-Noise Limits: What is the breakdown threshold of non-parametric delay embeddings when signal-to-noise ratio drops below 0 dB?
  3. Cross-Domain Generalization: How well does the architecture maintain its predictive edge across non-stationary geophysical, financial, and industrial streams?
